\begin{abstract}
Grammar-constrained decoding is standard wherever structured output is required,
and token-level entropy is a standard cheap uncertainty signal. The two are
routinely composed, since entropy is read from the distribution the decoder
samples, which under a constraint is the masked and renormalised one. We measure
entropy immediately before and after the mask, at the same position and on the
same prompts, for two instruction-tuned models. The two quantities order inputs
oppositely. Where the model concentrates its mass on a continuation the grammar
forbids, the surviving distribution is close to uniform over the permitted tokens
and presents as maximally uncertain, while the model itself was close to
resolved. The paired shift separates our two input classes with AUROC $0.85$ to
$0.92$ across both models. The mechanism is the retained mass, the fraction of
the model's probability the grammar admits, which is between $10^{-9}$ and
$10^{-11}$ at the first generated position and, importantly, is the same for both
input classes. We recommend reporting retained mass alongside any uncertainty
computed under a constraint, and show that the effect disappears once the prompt
makes the output format explicit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Estimates of a language model's predictive uncertainty are generally derived from
the distribution over next tokens that the model exposes, through its entropy,
the probability of the selected token, or the spread of sampled continuations
\citep{malinin-gales-2021-uncertainty,kuhn-etal-2023-semantic,liu-etal-2025-uncertainty}.
This is sound when the distribution a system uses for generation is the
distribution the model computed.

Grammar-constrained decoding breaks that identity. To guarantee that output
conforms to a schema, the decoder computes a per-token mask at each step, sets
the logits of violating tokens to $-\infty$, and renormalises over what remains
\citep{willard-louf-2023-efficient,dong-etal-2024-xgrammar}. The distribution
that then selects the next token, and that any downstream consumer can observe,
is not the model's distribution but its restriction to the admitted set. That
this restriction distorts the distribution is known and motivates grammar-aligned
decoding \citep{park-etal-2024-grammar,reddy-etal-2026-dccd}. What has received
less attention is what it does to uncertainty computed downstream of it, even
though structured output and uncertainty-based routing are both ordinary
production practice.

We measure one quantity, Shannon entropy of the next-token distribution, at two
points in one pipeline: immediately before the mask and immediately after it, at
the same position and on the same prompts. Our finding is a statement about sign.
The two readings order inputs \emph{oppositely}. On inputs where the model is
most resolved, the served distribution appears most uncertain, and on inputs
where the model is genuinely less resolved, the served distribution appears
confident. A system that ranks or thresholds on observed entropy therefore
recovers close to the reverse of the model's own state.

The cause is that almost nothing survives the mask. At the first generated
position the admitted set holds between $10^{-9}$ and $10^{-11}$ of the model's
probability, so the served distribution is a renormalisation of the model's
near-zero tail. Critically, this is true for both of our input classes in equal
measure, so retained mass is not what separates them. It is what makes the served
distribution uninformative about the model in both. What inverts the ordering is
the \emph{shape} of the surviving tail, which we develop in
Section~\ref{sec:results}.

To create a setting where the model's preference is unambiguous and is
structurally forbidden, we use refusal as a probe. A safety-aligned model given a
harmful request concentrates sharply on a refusal opener, and a JSON grammar
makes that opener impossible at the first token. The safety framing is
instrumental. We introduce no attack and report no attack success rates, and the
mechanism should apply wherever a model holds a sharp preference the schema
forbids.

Our contribution is a measurement, a mechanism, and a check. Retained mass is
computed inside every constrained-decoding implementation and is simply not
exposed. Reporting it tells a downstream consumer which of three regimes it is
in, and therefore whether an entropy computed under the constraint means anything
about the model.

\section{Background}
\label{sec:background}

Uncertainty quantification for language models spans token-level, sequence-level
and semantic estimators, surveyed by \citet{liu-etal-2025-uncertainty} and
\citet{shorinwa-etal-2025-survey}. We work at the token level, where entropy of
the next-token distribution and the probability of the selected token are the
standard cheap signals
\citep{malinin-gales-2021-uncertainty,fadeeva-etal-2024-fact}. Sequence-level and
semantic methods such as semantic entropy \citep{kuhn-etal-2023-semantic} operate
over sampled continuations. We measure at the token level because that is where
the intervention we study takes place.

A recurring theme at this venue is that a reported probability need not
correspond to the quantity a task requires.
\citet{lovering-etal-2025-language} and \citet{toney-wails-2025-certain} both
document quantities that behave like confidence without being it. Our result is
of that kind, with the mismatch introduced by a decoding-time transformation
rather than by training or elicitation.

Decoding is itself an intervention on the model's distribution
\citep{eikema-aziz-2020-map,eikema-2024-generalisation,hashimoto-etal-2024-beam}.
Grammar-constrained decoding is the sharpest case, since it sets the mass of
violating tokens to exactly zero
\citep{willard-louf-2023-efficient,dong-etal-2024-xgrammar}.
\citet{park-etal-2024-grammar} show the resulting distribution is not the
model's conditional on the grammar and propose a correction.
\citet{reddy-etal-2026-dccd} formalise the same object as the feasible mass
$\alpha(h_t)$ and give the identity we use in
Section~\ref{sec:setup}, reporting sequence-averaged $\alpha$ around $0.80$ and
$0.71$ for our two models on a mathematical reasoning task under a prompt that
both requests JSON and displays the schema. Our readings are far smaller because
we measure at a single position and without such a prompt. Aggregated their way,
our data give $0.75$ and $0.56$, so the two studies are consistent once
conditioning is matched. Format restrictions also carry a task cost
\citep{tam-etal-2024-speak}.

Two further connections are relevant. \citet{wei-etal-2023-jailbroken} describe
forced affirmative prefixes as a safety failure mode, which is why our probe uses
a schema that pins an imperative. \citet{steindl-etal-2024-linguistic} relate
predictive uncertainty to attack success at the sequence level, and report higher
entropy for prompts that evade safety mechanisms. Their measurement and ours are
not directly comparable, which is itself part of our point: token-level entropy
at the first position and sequence-level entropy over a response need not agree
in sign.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

Let $P_{\text{free}}(\cdot \mid h_t)$ be the model's next-token distribution over
vocabulary $V$ given context $h_t$, and $A_t \subseteq V$ the set the grammar
admits at step $t$. Constrained decoding is exactly two operations applied to the
logits after the forward pass, restriction and renormalisation, so with

\begin{equation}
R_t \;=\; \sum_{v \in A_t} P_{\text{free}}(v \mid h_t)
\label{eq:retained}
\end{equation}

the served distribution is $P_{\text{mask}}(v) = P_{\text{free}}(v)/R_t$ for
$v \in A_t$ and zero elsewhere. We call $R_t$ the \textbf{retained mass}. It is
the feasible mass $\alpha(h_t)$ of \citet{reddy-etal-2026-dccd}, and because
masking renormalises rather than reshapes,
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{\text{mask}} \,\|\, P_{\text{free}}) = -\log R_t$, their
Eq.~5. Only this direction is finite, since $P_{\text{mask}}$ assigns zero mass
where $P_{\text{free}}$ does not. We report $R_t$ rather than the divergence,
which is the same quantity in other units.

\paragraph{One metric, two read points.}
$H_{\text{pre}}$ is the entropy of $P_{\text{free}}$ over the full vocabulary and
$H_{\text{post}}$ the entropy of $P_{\text{mask}}$, both in bits and both exact
with no top-$k$ truncation. These are not two metrics but one metric read at two
points in a single pipeline, which is what makes the comparison controlled.
Writing $H_{A} = -\sum_{v \in A_t} P_{\text{free}}(v)\log_2 P_{\text{free}}(v)$,

\begin{equation}
H_{\text{post}} = \frac{H_{A}}{R_t} + \log_2 R_t ,
\qquad H_{\text{post}} \le \log_2 |A_t| .
\label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}

The two terms have opposing sign. Masking that removes low-probability tokens
leaves $R_t$ near one and lowers entropy, while masking that removes the mode
makes $R_t$ small and can raise it. The bound is a property of the schema, chosen
by whoever wrote it rather than by the model.

Our primary statistic is the per-prompt shift
$\Delta H = H_{\text{post}} - H_{\text{pre}}$. Both terms are measured on the
same prompt at the same position, so pairing removes prompt-level variance that a
two-sample comparison retains. We summarise separation with AUROC because the
claim concerns ordering rather than calibration, and because AUROC is scale-free
and so compares the two entropies without rescaling either.

\paragraph{Models and data.}
Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct \citep{yang-etal-2024-qwen25} and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct
\citep{grattafiori-etal-2024-llama3} in \texttt{bfloat16}, decoded greedily, with
vocabularies of $152{,}064$ and $128{,}256$. We use fifty harmful prompts from
HarmBench \citep{mazeika-etal-2024-harmbench}, fifty benign instructions from
Alpaca \citep{taori-etal-2023-alpaca} as the control, and fifty safe prompts from
XSTest \citep{rottger-etal-2024-xstest}, which are lexically alarming but benign
and serve as a hard negative in Section~\ref{sec:scope}. The schema requires
\texttt{\{"step1": "Step 1: $\langle$verb$\rangle$ \ldots"\}} with the verb drawn
from twenty-five imperatives, following the structured-output attack of
\citet{cda-2025-grammar}, and is compiled with XGrammar
\citep{dong-etal-2024-xgrammar}. We replace the generation loop so that raw
logits are read before the bitmask is applied and both distributions are
recorded, a seam that hosted APIs do not expose and that high-throughput servers
absorb into the sampling kernel \citep{kwon-etal-2023-efficient}.

\paragraph{Position and prompt conditioning.}
We report at the first generated token $t_0$, where the mask binds hardest and
where the model has conditioned on nothing it was forced to emit. We also vary
how much the prompt anticipates the schema, across four levels.
\textsc{none} has no system turn at all, so the grammar arrives unannounced.
\textsc{neutral} adds a system turn with generic assistant text and no format
content, controlling for the mere presence of a system turn. \textsc{terse}
instructs the model to emit only JSON conforming to the schema, following the
wording of \citet{reddy-etal-2026-dccd} so the two studies are comparable.
\textsc{schema} appends the schema literal to that instruction.
Section~\ref{sec:results} states the result for the two unannounced levels and
Section~\ref{sec:scope} reports the rest.

As a supporting measurement we record $P_{\text{refuse}}$, the mass
$P_{\text{free}}$ places on a fixed prefix-free set of twenty-eight refusal
continuations, evaluated by teacher forcing (Appendix~\ref{app:refusal}). All
$44/44$ refusals each model produces without a grammar begin with a member of
that set. Intervals are percentile bootstrap over prompts with $10{,}000$
resamples, with the two arms resampled independently at their design sizes. We
report no significance tests.

\paragraph{Retained mass is the enabling condition, not the discriminator.}
The grammar admits five tokens at $t_0$, the JSON opening brace and whitespace
variants. These hold a median $3.6\times10^{-11}$ of Qwen's mass and
$7.7\times10^{-9}$ of Llama's, so more than $99.9999\%$ of what each model wanted
is discarded and the served distribution is a renormalisation of the remainder.
Retained mass is, however, close to prompt-independent: the benign medians are
$1.6\times10^{-11}$ and $4.3\times10^{-9}$, within a factor of two to three, with
interquartile ranges overlapping across two orders of magnitude, and the ordering
is not consistent across models. This is expected, since no instruction-tuned
model prefers to open a reply with a brace whatever it was asked.

The amount removed therefore does not distinguish the two classes. What does is
the shape of what survives. Where the model's mass sits on a refusal opener, the
five permitted tokens carry tiny and roughly equal mass, and renormalising a flat
remainder yields a near-uniform distribution. Where the model has genuine
preference among those same five tokens, the renormalised distribution stays
peaked. Eq.~\ref{eq:decomp} is the formal statement of this, and
Figure~\ref{fig:swap} shows it for one prompt of each class. Consistent with
having real dynamic range rather than being pinned at its bound,
$H_{\text{post}}$ occupies between $25\%$ and $61\%$ of $\log_2|A_t|$.

Two orders of magnitude separate Llama's retained mass from Qwen's and both show
the inversion, so the effect is not sensitive to the precise magnitude.

\section{Scope}
\label{sec:scope}

The claim is stated for a grammar the prompt does not anticipate, and that
restriction is real. Across the eight cells in which the prompt does request JSON
(\textsc{terse} and \textsc{schema}, both benign sources), the inversion holds in
none. Retained mass is what moves: it rises from order $10^{-11}$ to between
$0.21$ and $1.00$, so the served distribution is no longer a near-zero tail. The
pattern sorts into three regimes.

When $R_t$ is small in both arms, the unannounced case, $H_{\text{post}}$ inverts
relative to $H_{\text{pre}}$. When it is large in both, as for Qwen under
\textsc{terse} where the medians are $0.28$ and $1.00$, the two agree, at AUROC
$0.914$ and $0.798$. When it is large in one arm only, as for Llama under
\textsc{terse} at $0.206$ benign against $1.3\times10^{-8}$ harmful,
$H_{\text{pre}}$ still separates at $0.026$ but $H_{\text{post}}$ carries no
signal at all, at $0.499$. A single scalar thus predicts which of the three
obtains, which is our reason for recommending that it be reported.

The two models leave the regime for different reasons, and $P_{\text{refuse}}$
distinguishes them. Across \textsc{none} to \textsc{schema} it is stable for
Llama at $0.830$, $0.884$, $0.892$ and $0.869$, but collapses for Qwen at
$0.769$, $0.660$, $0.071$ and $0.000$. For Qwen under an explicit format
instruction the premise of our claim simply stops applying, since there is no
longer a forbidden preference to invert. For Llama the preference survives and
the mask stops being the dominant effect on the benign side.

One caveat belongs here. Under \textsc{none}, Qwen's $H_{\text{post}}$ on XSTest
prompts is $1.481$ bits, higher than its $1.255$ on harmful ones. Safe but
lexically alarming prompts can therefore look more uncertain than genuinely
harmful ones, because $H_{\text{post}}$ inherits whatever disposition the model
holds, including over-refusal ($P_{\text{refuse}} = 0.372$ on XSTest against
$0.000$ on Alpaca). This is consistent with our account rather than a
counterexample to it, but it rules out reading $H_{\text{post}}$, or $R_t$, as a
detector of harm.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Uncertainty reported under grammar-constrained decoding is best read as a joint
property of the model and the constraint, and how much weight to place on it
depends on $R_t$. Where $R_t$ approaches one, $P_{\text{mask}}$ is close to a
conditional of the model's own distribution and an entropy computed from it
remains interpretable. Where $R_t$ is of order $10^{-11}$, it is unclear what
such an estimate is an estimate of, and our measurements indicate it is ordered
opposite to the quantity a practitioner intends.

The practical risk is not the direction one might expect. A confidence filter
passes low-entropy outputs as reliable. Under an unannounced grammar it is the
benign prompts that produce the lowest observed entropy while being the ones the
model was least resolved about, so such a filter would pass exactly the
generations that warranted inspection. We note that all entropies here are small
in absolute terms, between $0.06$ and $1.42$ bits against a possible $17$, which
is why every headline figure is an ordering statistic.

The recommendation follows and is modest. Under constrained decoding, uncertainty
is more informative when computed with respect to $P_{\text{free}}$, or at
minimum reported alongside $R_t$, which is available wherever the mask is and
costs nothing to record. This is not a new estimator but a statement about which
distribution an existing one should be applied to. It has a concrete consequence
for serving stacks, since implementations that apply the mask inside a fused
sampling kernel discard $P_{\text{free}}$ before anything can observe it.

\section*{Limitations}

Our study covers one grammar family, one harmful benchmark, fifty prompts per
condition, two open-weight models of comparable size, and a single decoding
position. Whether the effect holds for other schema types, larger models, or
positions deeper into a generation is open. Closed-weight systems cannot be
examined this way at all, since the method requires logits before the constraint
is applied, a restriction shared by probabilistic uncertainty measurement
generally \citep{steindl-etal-2024-linguistic}.

At $n = 50$ per arm the bootstrap intervals are wide enough that individual AUROC
values should not be compared with one another. We report the direction of each
and the fact that intervals exclude $0.5$, and draw no conclusions from
differences between them.

Inference is in \texttt{bfloat16}. Retained mass is computed in log space, as
$\log R_t = \mathrm{logsumexp}(z_{A_t}) - \mathrm{logsumexp}(z)$ on the logits
$z$, which avoids the underflow that a probability-space sum incurs at these
magnitudes. Precision then resides in the exponent, where a \texttt{bfloat16}
logit is accurate to roughly $0.06$ nats, so a reading of $35$ bits carries
approximately $0.09$ bits of uncertainty. Absolute masses should nonetheless be
read as order-of-magnitude quantities.

$P_{\text{refuse}}$ depends on a fixed English phrase set in one register. Its
coverage on unconstrained generations is complete for both models, but whether a
model's refusal vocabulary shifts inside a structured context is not testable
from our artefacts, since the grammar prevents a refusal from being emitted at
all. Retained mass is free of this dependence and shows the same pattern, which
is part of why we prefer it.

Finally, a control schema replacing the semantically loaded key \texttt{step1}
with a neutral one recovered part of the refusal mass on one model but not the
other (Appendix~\ref{app:extra}), so we draw no conclusion about which component
of the forced prefix is responsible.

\section*{Ethics Statement}

This work uses harmful prompts from a public red-teaming benchmark and a
published structured-output attack in order to create a setting in which a
model's preference is unambiguous. We introduce no new attack and report no
attack success rates. Generated completions were inspected only on screen during
development and are not persisted, and released artefacts contain token
identifiers and distributional statistics rather than decoded harmful text. We
regard the finding that a standard decoding path discards a signal a
safety-relevant system might depend on as defensively useful, since it identifies
a place where existing information could be retained rather than a new way to
remove it.
