\begin{abstract}
Token-level entropy is a standard cheap uncertainty signal, and
grammar-constrained decoding is standard wherever structured output is required.
The two are routinely composed, since entropy is read from the distribution the
decoder samples, which under a constraint is the masked and renormalised one. We
measure entropy immediately before and after the mask, at the same position and
on the same prompts, for two instruction-tuned models. The two readings order
inputs oppositely. Where the model concentrates on a continuation the grammar
forbids, the surviving distribution is close to uniform over the permitted tokens
and presents as undecided, while the model itself was close to resolved. The
cause is that the permitted set holds between $10^{-9}$ and $10^{-11}$ of the
model's probability, so the served distribution is a renormalisation of a
near-zero tail. We recommend reporting that retained mass alongside any
uncertainty computed under a constraint, and note that the effect disappears once
the prompt makes the output format explicit.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Predictive uncertainty for language models is generally derived from the
distribution over next tokens that the model exposes, through its entropy or a
monotone function of it
\citep{malinin-gales-2021-uncertainty,kuhn-etal-2023-semantic,liu-etal-2025-uncertainty}.
\citet{durai-2025-phases}, for instance, tracks confidence as
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p \,\|\, u) = \log|V| - H(p)$. This is sound whenever the
distribution a system uses for generation is the distribution the model computed.

Grammar-constrained decoding breaks that identity. To guarantee that output
conforms to a schema, the decoder computes a per-token mask at each step, sets
the logits of violating tokens to $-\infty$, and renormalises over what remains
\citep{willard-louf-2023-efficient,dong-etal-2024-xgrammar}. The distribution
that then selects the next token, and that any downstream consumer can observe,
is not the model's distribution but its restriction to the admitted set. That
this restriction distorts the distribution is known and motivates grammar-aligned
decoding \citep{park-etal-2024-grammar,reddy-etal-2026-dccd}. What it does to
uncertainty computed downstream of it has received less attention, although
structured output and uncertainty-based routing are both ordinary practice.

This paper reports one observation. We take a single metric, the entropy of the
next-token distribution, and read it at two points in one pipeline: immediately
before the mask and immediately after it, at the same position and on the same
prompts. The two readings order our two input classes \emph{oppositely}. On
inputs where the model is most resolved, the served distribution appears most
undecided, and on inputs where the model is genuinely less resolved, the served
distribution appears decisive. A system that ranks or thresholds on observed
entropy therefore recovers close to the reverse of the model's own state.

We follow \citet{steindl-etal-2024-linguistic} and
\citet{toney-wails-2025-certain} in reporting a mismatch between a quantity that
behaves like confidence and the thing it is taken to describe. Here the mismatch
is introduced by a decoding-time transformation rather than by training or
elicitation.

\section{What masking does to the distribution}
\label{sec:mechanism}

Let $P_{\text{free}}(\cdot \mid h_t)$ be the model's next-token distribution over
vocabulary $V$ given context $h_t$, and $A_t \subseteq V$ the set the grammar
admits at step $t$. Constrained decoding applies two operations to the logits
after the forward pass, restriction and renormalisation, so with

\begin{equation}
R_t \;=\; \sum_{v \in A_t} P_{\text{free}}(v \mid h_t)
\label{eq:retained}
\end{equation}

the served distribution is $P_{\text{mask}}(v) = P_{\text{free}}(v)/R_t$ on $A_t$
and zero elsewhere. We call $R_t$ the \textbf{retained mass}. It is the feasible
mass $\alpha(h_t)$ of \citet{reddy-etal-2026-dccd}, and because masking
renormalises rather than reshapes,
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{\text{mask}} \,\|\, P_{\text{free}}) = -\log R_t$.

Write $H_{\text{pre}}$ for the entropy of $P_{\text{free}}$ over the full
vocabulary and $H_{\text{post}}$ for the entropy of $P_{\text{mask}}$, both in
bits. These are one metric read at two points rather than two metrics, which is
what makes the comparison controlled. Writing
$H_{A} = -\sum_{v \in A_t} P_{\text{free}}(v)\log_2 P_{\text{free}}(v)$,

\begin{equation}
H_{\text{post}} = \frac{H_{A}}{R_t} + \log_2 R_t ,
\qquad H_{\text{post}} \le \log_2 |A_t| .
\label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}

The two terms have opposing sign. Masking that removes low-probability tokens
leaves $R_t$ near one and lowers entropy, which is the intuition that constraints
reduce uncertainty. Masking that removes the mode makes $R_t$ small and can raise
it instead. The upper bound is a property of the schema, fixed by whoever wrote
it rather than by the model.

The consequence we test is a claim about \emph{shape}. When the model's mass sits
on a continuation the grammar forbids, the admitted tokens carry little mass and
carry it in roughly equal amounts, so renormalising them yields something close
to uniform. When the model has genuine preference among the admitted tokens, that
preference survives renormalisation. The served distribution therefore reports
how indifferent the model was among the tokens the grammar happened to allow,
which need not track how resolved it was overall (Figure~\ref{fig:swap}).

\section{Measuring before and after}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Models and prompts.}
Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct \citep{yang-etal-2024-qwen25} and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct
\citep{grattafiori-etal-2024-llama3} in \texttt{bfloat16}, decoded greedily, with
vocabularies of $152{,}064$ and $128{,}256$. We use fifty harmful prompts from
HarmBench \citep{mazeika-etal-2024-harmbench} and fifty benign instructions from
Alpaca \citep{taori-etal-2023-alpaca}.

\paragraph{Constraint.}
The schema requires
\texttt{\{"step1": "Step 1: $\langle$verb$\rangle$ \ldots"\}} with the verb drawn
from twenty-five imperatives, following the structured-output attack of
\citet{cda-2025-grammar}, and is compiled with XGrammar
\citep{dong-etal-2024-xgrammar}. We replace the generation loop so that raw
logits are read before the bitmask is applied and both distributions are
recorded, a seam that hosted APIs do not expose and that high-throughput servers
absorb into the sampling kernel \citep{kwon-etal-2023-efficient}.

\paragraph{Position.}
We report at the first generated token, where the mask binds hardest and where
the model has conditioned on nothing it was forced to emit. The grammar admits
five tokens there, the JSON opening brace and whitespace variants, so
$H_{\text{post}} \le \log_2 5 = 2.32$ bits.

\paragraph{Why refusal.}
The claim needs a case where the model's preference is strong, identifiable, and
structurally forbidden. A safety-aligned model given a harmful request satisfies
all three, since it concentrates on a refusal opener and a JSON grammar makes
that opener impossible at the first token. The framing is instrumental. We
introduce no attack and report no attack success rates, and the mechanism should
apply wherever a model holds a sharp preference the schema forbids. To confirm
the preference is real we record $P_{\text{refuse}}$, the mass $P_{\text{free}}$
places on a fixed prefix-free set of twenty-eight refusal continuations, by
teacher forcing (Appendix~\ref{app:refusal}).

\paragraph{Prompt conditioning.}
Whether the prompt anticipates the schema turns out to matter, so we treat it as
a variable rather than leave it an unstated constant. \textsc{none} has no system
turn, so the grammar arrives unannounced. \textsc{neutral} adds a system turn
with generic assistant text and no format content, controlling for the presence
of a system turn. Section~\ref{sec:scope} reports two further levels in which the
prompt does request JSON.

\paragraph{The model is resolved where the served distribution is not.}
On harmful prompts $H_{\text{pre}}$ is $0.06$ to $0.21$ bits
(Table~\ref{tab:main}), which bounds the most probable token below by $96\%$ of
the mass in every cell. $P_{\text{refuse}}$ of $0.77$ and $0.83$ identifies what
that mass is on, and without a grammar both models open with a refusal on $88\%$
of these prompts. On benign prompts the model is markedly less resolved, at
$0.76$ to $0.93$ bits. The served distribution reverses this in every cell,
carrying more entropy on harmful prompts than on benign ones.

\paragraph{The shift is large and consistent.}
Masking adds $0.58$ to $1.36$ bits of apparent uncertainty on harmful prompts. On
benign prompts it subtracts for Llama and adds a little for Qwen, so the
model-general statement concerns magnitude rather than sign. Taking the per-prompt
shift $\Delta H = H_{\text{post}} - H_{\text{pre}}$, which is paired because both
terms are measured on the same prompt at the same position, harmful and benign
prompts separate at AUROC $0.845$ to $0.916$ across the four cells, with $95\%$
bootstrap intervals excluding $0.5$ in all of them.

\paragraph{Retained mass is the enabling condition, not the discriminator.}
The five admitted tokens hold a median $3.6\times10^{-11}$ of Qwen's mass and
$7.7\times10^{-9}$ of Llama's on harmful prompts, so the served distribution is a
renormalisation of a remainder the model had all but ruled out. Retained mass is
however close to prompt-independent. The benign medians are
$1.6\times10^{-11}$ and $4.3\times10^{-9}$, within a factor of two to three, with
interquartile ranges overlapping across two orders of magnitude and no consistent
ordering across models. This is expected, since no instruction-tuned model
prefers to open a reply with a brace whatever it was asked.

The amount removed therefore does not distinguish the two classes. What does is
the shape of what survives, as Eq.~\ref{eq:decomp} describes and
Figure~\ref{fig:swap} shows. Consistent with having real dynamic range rather
than being pinned at its bound, $H_{\text{post}}$ occupies between $25\%$ and
$61\%$ of $\log_2|A_t|$. Two orders of magnitude separate Llama's retained mass
from Qwen's and both show the inversion, so the effect does not depend on the
precise magnitude.

\section{Scope and discussion}
\label{sec:scope}

The observation is stated for a grammar the prompt does not anticipate, and that
restriction is real. Across the eight cells in which the prompt does request JSON,
the inversion holds in none. Retained mass is what moves, rising from order
$10^{-11}$ to between $0.21$ and $1.00$, so the served distribution is no longer a
near-zero tail and $H_{\text{post}}$ becomes interpretable again. The two models
leave the regime differently. Llama keeps its refusal disposition across all four
prompt levels, at $P_{\text{refuse}}$ of $0.83$ to $0.89$, whereas Qwen's
collapses from $0.77$ to $0.00$, so for Qwen there is no longer a forbidden
preference to invert. Appendix~\ref{app:extra} reports these cells in full,
together with a harder benign control drawn from XSTest
\citep{rottger-etal-2024-xstest} on which $H_{\text{post}}$ is higher than on
harmful prompts, which rules out reading either quantity as a detector of harm.

Uncertainty reported under grammar-constrained decoding is therefore best read as
a joint property of the model and the constraint, with $R_t$ indicating how much
weight it can bear. Where $R_t$ approaches one, $P_{\text{mask}}$ is close to a
conditional of the model's own distribution and an entropy computed from it
remains meaningful. Where $R_t$ is of order $10^{-11}$, our measurements indicate
it is ordered opposite to the quantity a practitioner intends.

The practical risk is not the direction one might expect. A confidence filter
passes low-entropy outputs as reliable. Under an unannounced grammar it is the
benign prompts that produce the lowest observed entropy while being the ones the
model was least resolved about, so such a filter would pass exactly the
generations that warranted inspection. All entropies here are small in absolute
terms, between $0.06$ and $1.42$ bits against a possible $17$, which is why the
claim concerns ordering rather than absolute level.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Reading entropy before and after a grammar mask at the same position, we find the
two order inputs oppositely whenever the mask removes the model's preferred
continuation. The recommendation is modest and cheap. Under constrained decoding,
uncertainty is more informative computed with respect to $P_{\text{free}}$, or at
minimum reported alongside $R_t$, which is available wherever the mask is. This
is not a new estimator but a statement about which distribution an existing one
should be applied to. It has a concrete consequence for serving stacks, since
implementations that apply the mask inside a fused sampling kernel discard
$P_{\text{free}}$ before anything can observe it.

\section*{Limitations}

Our study covers one grammar family, one harmful benchmark, fifty prompts per
condition, two open-weight models of comparable size, and a single decoding
position. Whether the effect holds for other schema types, larger models, or
positions deeper into a generation is open. Closed-weight systems cannot be
examined this way at all, since the method requires logits before the constraint
is applied, a restriction shared by probabilistic uncertainty measurement
generally \citep{steindl-etal-2024-linguistic}.

At fifty prompts per arm the bootstrap intervals are wide enough that individual
AUROC values should not be compared with one another. We report the direction of
each and the fact that intervals exclude $0.5$, and draw no conclusion from
differences between them. The marginal reading of $H_{\text{post}}$ alone is
weaker than the paired shift, and in one of the four cells its interval reaches
$0.5$ (Appendix~\ref{app:extra}).

Inference is in \texttt{bfloat16}. Retained mass is computed in log space, as
$\log R_t = \mathrm{logsumexp}(z_{A_t}) - \mathrm{logsumexp}(z)$ on the logits
$z$, which avoids the underflow a probability-space sum incurs at these
magnitudes. Precision then resides in the exponent, where a \texttt{bfloat16}
logit is accurate to roughly $0.06$ nats, so a reading of $35$ bits carries
approximately $0.09$ bits of uncertainty. Absolute masses should be read as
order-of-magnitude quantities.

$P_{\text{refuse}}$ depends on a fixed English phrase set in one register. Its
coverage on unconstrained generations is complete for both models, but whether a
model's refusal vocabulary shifts inside a structured context is not testable
from our artefacts, since the grammar prevents a refusal from being emitted at
all. Retained mass is free of this dependence and shows the same pattern.

\section*{Ethics Statement}

This work uses harmful prompts from a public red-teaming benchmark and a
published structured-output attack in order to create a setting in which a
model's preference is unambiguous. We introduce no new attack and report no
attack success rates. Generated completions were inspected only on screen during
development and are not persisted, and released artefacts contain token
identifiers and distributional statistics rather than decoded harmful text. We
regard the finding that a standard decoding path discards a signal a
safety-relevant system might depend on as defensively useful, since it identifies
a place where existing information could be retained rather than a new way to
remove it.
