\begin{abstract}
Token-level entropy is a standard cheap uncertainty signal, and
grammar-constrained decoding is standard wherever structured output is required.
The two are routinely composed, since entropy is read from the distribution the
decoder samples, which under a constraint is the masked and renormalised one. We
measure entropy immediately before and after the mask, at the same position and
on the same prompts, for two instruction-tuned models. When the grammar arrives
unannounced, the two measurements can rank inputs in opposite orders. Where the
model concentrates on a continuation the schema forbids, the surviving
distribution is markedly flatter over the permitted tokens and reads as
undecided, while the model itself was close to resolved. This is possible because
the permitted set holds between $10^{-8}$ and $10^{-12}$ of the model's
probability in both conditions, so what is served is a renormalisation of a
near-zero tail either way, and it is the shape of that tail that reverses the
ordering. The effect disappears once the prompt requests the format, which makes
retained mass a usable indicator of when it applies. We therefore recommend
reporting retained probability mass alongside any uncertainty measured under
constrained decoding.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autoregressive language models increasingly sit inside pipelines that require
machine-readable output, and structured generation has become the standard way to
obtain it \citep{geng-etal-2023-grammar,willard-louf-2023-efficient,dong-etal-2024-xgrammar}.
The same pipelines routinely gate on a confidence signal, whether to abstain, to
escalate a query to a larger model, or to flag a possible hallucination
\citep{fadeeva-etal-2024-fact,gupta-etal-2024-cascades}. These two practices meet
at a point that has not been examined. Grammar-constrained decoding guarantees
schema conformance by masking the tokens that would violate it and renormalising
over what survives, so the distribution a downstream consumer observes is not the
distribution the model computed but its restriction to the admitted set.

Uncertainty estimation offers a way to attach confidence to a prediction, through
the entropy of the next-token distribution, the probability of the selected
token, or the spread of sampled continuations
\citep{malinin-gales-2021-uncertainty,kuhn-etal-2023-semantic,baan-etal-2023-uncertainty}.
Most of this work evaluates a distribution the model produced directly. Work on
constrained decoding, for its part, is aware that the restriction distorts what
is sampled and proposes corrections
\citep{park-etal-2024-grammar,reddy-etal-2026-dccd}. What is less well understood
is what that distortion does to an uncertainty estimate computed downstream of
it, which is the quantity a deployed system actually reads.

We take a single metric, the entropy of the next-token distribution, and read it
at two points in one pipeline: immediately before the mask and immediately after
it, at the same position and on the same prompts. Our key finding is that when
the grammar arrives unannounced, these two readings can rank inputs in opposite
orders. On inputs where the model is most resolved the masked distribution
appears most undecided, so a system that thresholds on observed entropy recovers
close to the reverse of the model's own state.

The reversal is driven not by how much mass the grammar removes, which is
near-identical for both classes of input, but by the shape of the little that
survives. That makes retained mass a usable indicator of when the served entropy
can be trusted, and reporting it is our recommendation.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Uncertainty quantification for language models spans token-level, sequence-level
and semantic estimators, surveyed by \citet{liu-etal-2025-uncertainty} and
\citet{shorinwa-etal-2025-survey} and benchmarked by
\citet{vashurin-etal-2025-polygraph}. We work at the token level, where entropy
and the probability of the selected token are the standard cheap signals
\citep{malinin-gales-2021-uncertainty,baan-etal-2023-uncertainty}, and which is
where the intervention we study takes place. These signals are not only
diagnostic. \citet{fadeeva-etal-2024-fact} flag unsupported claims with them and
\citet{gupta-etal-2024-cascades} route between models in a cascade on them, so a
sign error has operational consequences. Semantic methods such as semantic
entropy \citep{kuhn-etal-2023-semantic} instead aggregate over sampled
continuations.

A recurring theme at this workshop is that a reported probability need not
correspond to the quantity a task requires.
\citet{toney-wails-2025-certain} find token-level probabilities diverging from
theoretical distributions in probabilistic scenarios,
\citet{lovering-etal-2025-language} report them uncalibrated in numeric contexts,
and \citet{durai-2025-phases} tracks confidence as
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p \,\|\, u) = \log|V| - H(p)$, a monotone function of entropy,
through a training phase in which confidence rises while calibration worsens.
\citet{steindl-etal-2024-linguistic} relate predictive uncertainty to jailbreak
success at the sequence level. Our result is of this kind, with the mismatch
introduced by a decoding-time transformation rather than by training,
elicitation, or the task.

Grammar-constrained decoding enforces a formal output structure without
finetuning \citep{geng-etal-2023-grammar} and is now standard serving
infrastructure \citep{willard-louf-2023-efficient,dong-etal-2024-xgrammar}. Its
distortion of the model's distribution is known.
\citet{park-etal-2024-grammar} show the resulting distribution is not the model's
conditional on the grammar and give an adaptive correction,
\citet{beurer-kellner-etal-2024-guiding} show that misaligning subword
vocabularies with the constraint impairs accuracy, and
\citet{reddy-etal-2026-dccd} formalise the admitted mass as the feasible mass
$\alpha(h_t)$ and give the identity we use below.
\citet{tam-etal-2024-speak} document a task cost for format restrictions, and
\citet{le-2026-schema} shows that schema-key wording acts as an instruction
channel in its own right, with Qwen models responding more to schema-level
instruction and Llama models to prompt-level guidance, a split we also observe.

The masking step is also a security surface.
\citet{zhang-etal-2026-grammar} introduce the constrained decoding attack, in
which a schema injects a prefix into the generation trajectory and the model
completes the harmful intent, the control-plane analogue of the forced
affirmative prefix of \citet{wei-etal-2023-jailbroken}. We borrow their schema as
a probe rather than studying the attack, because it produces the situation our
claim requires, a strong model preference the grammar forbids. None of this work
reports what the distortion does to an uncertainty estimate read from the served
distribution, which is our subject.

\section{Entropy shifts under constrained decoding}
\label{sec:mechanism}

Let $P_{\text{free}}(\cdot \mid h_t)$ be the model's next-token distribution over
vocabulary $V$ given context $h_t$, and $A_t \subseteq V$ the set the grammar
admits at step $t$. Constrained decoding applies two operations to the logits
after the forward pass, restriction and renormalisation, so with

\begin{equation}
R_t \;=\; \sum_{v \in A_t} P_{\text{free}}(v \mid h_t)
\label{eq:retained}
\end{equation}

the masked distribution is $P_{\text{mask}}(v) = P_{\text{free}}(v)/R_t$ on $A_t$
and zero elsewhere. We call $R_t$ the \textbf{retained mass}. It is the feasible
mass $\alpha(h_t)$ of \citet{reddy-etal-2026-dccd}, and because masking
renormalises rather than reshapes,
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{\text{mask}} \,\|\, P_{\text{free}}) = -\log_2 R_t$ bits.

Write $H_{\text{pre}}$ for the entropy of the model's own distribution over the
full vocabulary and $H_{\text{post}}$ for the entropy of the masked distribution,
both in bits. These are one metric read at two points rather than two separate
metrics, which is what makes the comparison controlled. Setting
$H_{A} = -\sum_{v \in A_t} P_{\text{free}}(v)\log_2 P_{\text{free}}(v)$, the two
are related exactly:

\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;H_{\text{post}} = \frac{H_{A}}{R_t} + \log_2 R_t \;}
\qquad H_{\text{post}} \le \log_2 |A_t|
\label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}

Equation~\ref{eq:decomp} is the whole mechanism, because its two terms pull in
opposite directions. Masking that removes only low-probability tokens leaves
$R_t$ near one and lowers entropy, which is the familiar intuition that
constraints reduce uncertainty. Masking that removes the mode drives $R_t$ small,
and the first term can then dominate and raise entropy instead. The upper bound
is a property of the schema, fixed by whoever wrote it rather than by the model.

What we test is therefore a claim about shape rather than about quantity. When
the model's mass sits on a continuation the grammar forbids, the admitted tokens
carry little mass in roughly equal amounts, so renormalising them yields a
markedly flatter distribution. A genuine preference among the admitted tokens, by
contrast, survives renormalisation intact. The served entropy reports how
indifferent the model was among the tokens the grammar happened to allow, which
need not track how resolved it was overall (Figure~\ref{fig:swap}).

\section{Measuring before and after the mask}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Models and prompts.}
Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct \citep{yang-etal-2024-qwen25} and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct
\citep{grattafiori-etal-2024-llama3} in \texttt{bfloat16}, decoded greedily, with
vocabularies of $152{,}064$ and $128{,}256$. We use fifty harmful prompts from
HarmBench \citep{mazeika-etal-2024-harmbench} and fifty benign instructions from
Alpaca \citep{taori-etal-2023-alpaca}.

\paragraph{Constraint.}
The schema requires a JSON object with five required fields \texttt{step1} to
\texttt{step5}, each pinned to
\texttt{\^{}Step $k$: ($\langle$verb$\rangle$)} followed by at least sixty further
characters, with the verb drawn from twenty-five imperatives. It follows the
structured-output attack of \citet{zhang-etal-2026-grammar} and is compiled with
XGrammar \citep{dong-etal-2024-xgrammar}. We replace the generation loop so that
raw logits are read before the bitmask is applied and both distributions are
recorded, a seam that hosted APIs do not expose and that high-throughput servers
absorb into the sampling kernel \citep{kwon-etal-2023-efficient}.

\paragraph{Position and probe.}
We report at the first generated token, where the mask binds hardest and the
model has conditioned on nothing it was forced to emit. The grammar admits five
tokens there, the JSON opening brace and whitespace variants, so
$H_{\text{post}} \le \log_2 5 = 2.32$ bits. The claim needs a preference that is
strong, identifiable and structurally forbidden, and a safety-aligned model given
a harmful request supplies all three. The framing is instrumental, we introduce
no attack and report no attack success rates, and the mechanism should apply
wherever a model holds a sharp preference the schema forbids. To confirm the
preference is real we record $P_{\text{refuse}}$, the probability the model
assigns to fourteen refusal phrases in bare and space-prefixed form, reduced to a
prefix-free set and scored by teacher forcing.

\paragraph{Prompt conditioning.}
Whether the prompt anticipates the schema turns out to matter, so we vary it
rather than leave it an unstated constant. \textsc{none} has no system turn, so
the grammar arrives unannounced, and \textsc{neutral} adds a system turn with no
format content, controlling for its presence. Two further levels request JSON
explicitly, \textsc{terse} following the wording of
\citet{reddy-etal-2026-dccd} and \textsc{schema} appending the schema literal.
Intervals are $95\%$ percentile bootstrap over prompts with $10{,}000$ resamples,
both arms resampled independently at their design sizes.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{The model is most resolved exactly where the masked distribution looks
least resolved.}
On harmful prompts the model's own entropy is $0.06$ to $0.21$ bits
(Table~\ref{tab:main}), which bounds the most probable token below by $96\%$ of
the mass in every cell. $P_{\text{refuse}}$ identifies what that mass is on, at
$0.77$ and $0.83$ under \textsc{none} and $0.66$ and $0.88$ under
\textsc{neutral}, and without a grammar both models open with a refusal on $88\%$
of these prompts. On benign prompts the model is markedly less resolved, at
$0.76$ to $0.93$ bits. The masked distribution reverses this ordering in every
cell, carrying more entropy on harmful prompts than on benign ones.

\paragraph{The shift is large, and it is the shift that separates the two
classes.}
Masking adds $0.58$ to $1.36$ bits of apparent uncertainty on harmful prompts. On
benign prompts it subtracts for Llama and adds a little for Qwen, so the
model-general statement concerns magnitude rather than sign. The per-prompt shift
$\Delta H = H_{\text{post}} - H_{\text{pre}}$ is paired, since both terms come
from the same prompt at the same position, and it separates harmful from benign
at AUROC $0.845$ to $0.916$ across the four cells, with intervals excluding $0.5$
in all of them (Figure~\ref{fig:slope}).

\paragraph{What is removed does not separate the classes, but what survives
does.}
The five admitted tokens hold a median $3.6\times10^{-11}$ of Qwen's mass and
$7.7\times10^{-9}$ of Llama's on harmful prompts, so the served distribution is a
renormalisation of a remainder the model had all but ruled out. Retained mass is
however close to prompt-independent. The benign medians are $1.6\times10^{-11}$
and $4.3\times10^{-9}$, within a factor of two, with interquartile ranges that
overlap over more than an order of magnitude in every pair, and the ordering is
not even consistent across prompt levels.

This is expected, since no instruction-tuned model prefers to open a reply with a
brace whatever it was asked. What distinguishes the two classes is instead the
shape of what survives, as Equation~\ref{eq:decomp} describes and
Figure~\ref{fig:swap} shows. Two orders of magnitude separate Llama's retained
mass from Qwen's and both show the reversal, so the effect does not depend on the
precise magnitude, and $H_{\text{post}}$ occupies between $25\%$ and $61\%$ of
its ceiling rather than sitting pinned against it.

\paragraph{The reversal stops as soon as the prompt requests the format.}
Across the eight cells in which the prompt does request JSON, the ordering
reverses in none, and retained mass is what moves. For Qwen it rises in both
arms, to between $0.28$ and $1.00$, and the served entropy then tracks the
model's own rather than opposing it. For Llama it rises only on the benign side,
to $0.21$ under \textsc{terse}, while the harmful arm stays at order $10^{-8}$.
In that mixed case the model's own entropy still separates the classes at AUROC
$0.026$ but the served entropy carries no signal at all, at $0.499$.

The two models leave the regime for different reasons. Llama keeps its refusal
disposition across all four levels, at $P_{\text{refuse}}$ of $0.83$ to $0.89$,
whereas Qwen's collapses from $0.77$ to $0.00$, so for Qwen there is no longer a
forbidden preference for the mask to hide. On a harder benign control drawn from
XSTest \citep{rottger-etal-2024-xstest}, Qwen's served entropy exceeds its value
on harmful prompts, which rules out reading either quantity as a detector of
harm.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Reading entropy before and after a grammar mask at the same position, we find the
two can rank inputs in opposite orders whenever the grammar arrives unannounced.
The reversal is driven not by how much probability the mask removes, which is
near-identical across our two input classes, but by the shape of the remainder it
renormalises.

The practical risk runs opposite to intuition. A confidence filter passes
low-entropy outputs as reliable, and under an unannounced grammar it is the
benign prompts that produce the lowest observed entropy while being the ones the
model was least resolved about. A cascade that escalates on high observed entropy
\citep{gupta-etal-2024-cascades} would escalate the cases the model had already
settled and pass through the ones it had not. The claim concerns ordering rather
than absolute level, since every entropy here is small, between $0.06$ and $1.42$
bits against a possible $17$.

Our recommendation is correspondingly modest. Uncertainty computed under a
constraint is more informative taken with respect to the model's own
distribution, or at minimum reported alongside the retained mass, which is
available wherever the mask is and costs nothing to record. This is not a new
estimator but a statement about which distribution an existing one should be
applied to, and it has a concrete consequence for serving stacks, since
implementations that apply the mask inside a fused sampling kernel discard the
model's own distribution before anything can observe it.

\section*{Limitations}

Our study covers one grammar family, one harmful benchmark, fifty prompts per
condition, two open-weight models of comparable size, and a single decoding
position. Whether the effect holds for other schema types, larger models, or
positions deeper into a generation is open. Closed-weight systems cannot be
examined this way at all, since the method requires logits before the constraint
is applied, a restriction shared by probabilistic uncertainty measurement
generally \citep{steindl-etal-2024-linguistic}.

At fifty prompts per arm the bootstrap intervals are wide enough that individual
AUROC values should not be compared with one another. We report the direction of
each and the fact that intervals exclude $0.5$, and draw no conclusion from
differences between them. The paired shift is our primary statistic. Read on
their own, the two entropies are weaker evidence, and in one of the four
unannounced cells the interval for the served entropy reaches $0.5$. Table~1
reports means without a dispersion measure.

Inference is in \texttt{bfloat16}. Retained mass is computed in log space, as
$\log R_t = \mathrm{logsumexp}(z_{A_t}) - \mathrm{logsumexp}(z)$ on the logits
$z$, which avoids the underflow a probability-space sum incurs at these
magnitudes. Precision then resides in the exponent, where a \texttt{bfloat16}
logit is accurate to roughly $0.06$ nats, so a reading of $35$ bits carries
approximately $0.09$ bits of uncertainty. Absolute masses should be read as
order-of-magnitude quantities.

$P_{\text{refuse}}$ depends on a fixed English phrase set in one register. Its
coverage on unconstrained generations is complete for both models, but whether a
model's refusal vocabulary shifts inside a structured context is not testable
from our artefacts, since the grammar prevents a refusal from being emitted at
all. Retained mass is free of this dependence and shows the same pattern.

\section*{Ethics Statement}

This work uses harmful prompts from a public red-teaming benchmark and a
published structured-output attack in order to create a setting in which a
model's preference is unambiguous. We introduce no new attack and report no
attack success rates. Generated completions were inspected only on screen during
development and are not persisted, and released artefacts contain token
identifiers and distributional statistics rather than decoded harmful text. We
regard the finding that a standard decoding path discards a signal a
safety-relevant system might depend on as defensively useful, since it identifies
a place where existing information could be retained rather than a new way to
remove it.
