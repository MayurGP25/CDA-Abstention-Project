\begin{abstract}
Token-level entropy is a cheap and widely used uncertainty signal. Constrained
decoding is standard wherever a system needs structured output. The two are
routinely composed, because entropy is read off the distribution the decoder
samples, and under a constraint that is the masked one. We read entropy at two
points in the same pipeline, immediately before the mask and immediately after
it, at the same position and on the same prompts. When the grammar arrives
unannounced, the two readings can order inputs oppositely. On prompts where the
model is most decided, the surviving distribution looks least decided. The mask
removes the same amount of probability either way, between $10^{-8}$ and
$10^{-12}$, so what reverses the ordering is the shape of what is left rather
than how much was taken. The effect disappears once the prompt asks for the
format, which makes retained mass a usable indicator of when it applies. We
recommend reporting retained probability mass alongside any uncertainty measured
under constrained decoding.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Structured generation has become routine. Models are asked for JSON, for function
calls, for tool arguments, and grammar-constrained decoding is how systems
guarantee the output parses
\citep{geng-etal-2023-grammar,willard-louf-2023-efficient,dong-etal-2024-xgrammar}.
Those same systems often gate on a confidence signal, either to abstain, to
escalate a query, or to flag a possible hallucination
\citep{fadeeva-etal-2024-fact,gupta-etal-2024-cascades}. Both practices are
ordinary, and their interaction is not well understood. The reason it matters is
mechanical. A grammar mask sets the logits of violating tokens to $-\infty$ and
renormalises what remains, so the distribution a downstream component can observe
is not the one the model produced. It is that distribution restricted to whatever
the schema allows. Prior work knows this restriction distorts sampling and
proposes corrections for it
\citep{park-etal-2024-grammar,reddy-etal-2026-dccd}. Nobody has asked what it does
to an uncertainty estimate computed afterwards.

That question is for this community in particular. Entropy, perplexity and their
monotone transforms are the instruments we use to decide when a model should be
trusted. Our objective is to establish whether one of them still measures what it
is taken to measure after a decoding-time transformation, and if not, to give a
cheap way of telling.

We take a single metric, the entropy of the next-token distribution, and read it
twice: once from the model's own distribution and once from the masked one, at
the same position and on the same prompts. When the grammar arrives unannounced
the two readings can order inputs oppositely. Prompts on which the model has all
but settled produce the highest observed entropy, so a system thresholding on
what it can see recovers close to the reverse of the model's own state. What
drives this is not the volume of probability the mask removes, which is almost
identical for the two kinds of input we compare, but the shape of the small
remainder that gets renormalised. Retained mass is therefore a diagnostic rather
than an explanation. Where it is vanishing, the served entropy describes the
schema more than the model. Where it is not, the two readings agree again.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Uncertainty quantification for language models spans token-level, sequence-level
and semantic estimators
\citep{liu-etal-2025-uncertainty,shorinwa-etal-2025-survey,vashurin-etal-2025-polygraph}.
We work at the token level, where entropy is the standard cheap signal
\citep{malinin-gales-2021-uncertainty,baan-etal-2023-uncertainty} and where the
intervention we study happens. It is also used operationally, not only as a
diagnostic. \citet{fadeeva-etal-2024-fact} flag unsupported claims with it and
\citet{gupta-etal-2024-cascades} route model cascades on it, so an error in its
sign has consequences downstream. Semantic methods such as semantic entropy
\citep{kuhn-etal-2023-semantic} aggregate over sampled continuations instead.

A recurring theme at this workshop is that a reported probability need not match
the quantity a task requires. \citet{toney-wails-2025-certain} find token-level
probabilities diverging from theoretical distributions,
\citet{lovering-etal-2025-language} find them uncalibrated in numeric contexts,
and \citet{durai-2025-phases} tracks confidence as
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p \,\|\, u) = \log|V| - H(p)$ through a training phase where it
rises while calibration worsens. \citet{steindl-etal-2024-linguistic} relate
predictive uncertainty to jailbreak success at the sequence level. Ours is a
result of the same kind, with the mismatch introduced at decoding time rather
than during training or elicitation.

On the decoding side, grammar-constrained decoding enforces structure without
finetuning \citep{geng-etal-2023-grammar} and is now standard serving
infrastructure \citep{willard-louf-2023-efficient,dong-etal-2024-xgrammar}. Its
distortion of the model's distribution is documented.
\citet{park-etal-2024-grammar} show the constrained distribution is not the
model's conditional on the grammar and give a correction.
\citet{beurer-kellner-etal-2024-guiding} show that misaligning subword
vocabularies with a constraint costs accuracy. And
\citet{reddy-etal-2026-dccd} formalise the admitted mass as feasible mass
$\alpha(h_t)$, giving the identity we use in Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}.
\citet{tam-etal-2024-speak} measure a task cost for format restrictions.
\citet{le-2026-schema} shows schema keys act as an instruction channel, with Qwen
models responding to schema-level instruction and Llama models to prompt-level
guidance, a split we also see.

The mask is a security surface too. \citet{zhang-etal-2026-grammar} introduce the
constrained decoding attack, where a schema injects a prefix and the model
completes the harmful intent, which is the control-plane analogue of the forced
affirmative prefix of \citet{wei-etal-2023-jailbroken}. We borrow their schema as
a probe rather than studying the attack, because it produces the situation our
question needs: a strong model preference the grammar forbids.

\section{Entropy under a grammar mask}
\label{sec:mechanism}

Let $P_{\text{free}}(\cdot \mid h_t)$ be the model's next-token distribution over
vocabulary $V$, and $A_t \subseteq V$ the set the grammar admits at step $t$.
Masking restricts and renormalises, so writing

\begin{equation}
R_t \;=\; \sum_{v \in A_t} P_{\text{free}}(v \mid h_t)
\label{eq:retained}
\end{equation}

for the \textbf{retained mass}, the masked distribution is
$P_{\text{mask}}(v) = P_{\text{free}}(v)/R_t$ on $A_t$ and zero elsewhere. This is
the feasible mass $\alpha(h_t)$ of \citet{reddy-etal-2026-dccd}. Because masking
renormalises rather than reshapes,
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{\text{mask}} \,\|\, P_{\text{free}}) = -\log_2 R_t$ bits.

Write $H_{\text{pre}}$ for the entropy of the model's own distribution and
$H_{\text{post}}$ for that of the masked one, both in bits. These are one metric
read at two points, not two metrics, which is what makes the comparison
controlled. Setting
$H_{A} = -\sum_{v \in A_t} P_{\text{free}}(v)\log_2 P_{\text{free}}(v)$, the two
are related exactly:

\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;H_{\text{post}} = \frac{H_{A}}{R_t} + \log_2 R_t \;}
\qquad H_{\text{post}} \le \log_2 |A_t|
\label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}

Equation~\ref{eq:decomp} is the whole mechanism, and its two terms pull opposite
ways. Mask only low-probability tokens and $R_t$ stays near one, entropy falls,
and the familiar intuition holds that constraints reduce uncertainty. Mask the
mode and $R_t$ becomes small, at which point the first term can dominate and
entropy rises instead. The bound is set by whoever wrote the schema, not by the
model.

So the question is about shape, not quantity. When the model's mass sits on a
continuation the schema forbids, the admitted tokens hold little of it and hold
it in roughly equal amounts, and renormalising a flat remainder gives a flat
distribution. A real preference among the admitted tokens survives
renormalisation untouched. The served entropy reports how indifferent the model
was among whatever the grammar happened to allow, which need not track how
settled it was about what to say.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Models and prompts.}
Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct \citep{yang-etal-2024-qwen25} and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct
\citep{grattafiori-etal-2024-llama3} in \texttt{bfloat16}, decoded greedily, with
vocabularies of $152{,}064$ and $128{,}256$. We use fifty harmful prompts from
HarmBench \citep{mazeika-etal-2024-harmbench} and fifty benign instructions from
Alpaca \citep{taori-etal-2023-alpaca}.

\paragraph{Constraint.}
The schema is a JSON object with five required fields \texttt{step1} to
\texttt{step5}, each pinned to \texttt{\^{}Step $k$: ($\langle$verb$\rangle$)} and
at least sixty further characters, the verb drawn from twenty-five imperatives. It
follows \citet{zhang-etal-2026-grammar} and compiles with XGrammar
\citep{dong-etal-2024-xgrammar}. We replace the decoding loop so raw logits are
read before the bitmask is applied and both distributions are recorded. That seam
is not exposed by hosted APIs and is absorbed into the sampling kernel by
high-throughput servers \citep{kwon-etal-2023-efficient}.

\paragraph{Position.}
We measure at the first generated token. The mask binds hardest there, and the
model has not yet conditioned on anything it was forced to emit. At that point the
matcher sits in its initial state, so the admitted set depends on the grammar
alone and is the same five tokens for every prompt. Differences in
$H_{\text{post}}$ cannot come from differences in what was permitted, and
$H_{\text{post}} \le \log_2 5 = 2.32$ bits. Those five are the opening brace and
byte-pair variants carrying trailing whitespace, which is one semantic choice
split across ways of writing it. Note also that the two entropies live on
different supports, the full vocabulary against these five, so we treat their
difference as a per-prompt summary and phrase the claim as one about ordering.

\paragraph{Probe and conditioning.}
The claim needs a preference that is strong, identifiable and structurally
forbidden. A safety-aligned model given a harmful request supplies all three. The
framing is instrumental: we introduce no attack, report no attack success rates,
and expect the mechanism wherever a model holds a sharp preference the schema
forbids. To check the preference is real we record $P_{\text{refuse}}$, the
probability the model assigns to fourteen refusal phrases in bare and
space-prefixed form, reduced to a prefix-free set and scored by teacher forcing.
Whether the prompt anticipates the schema turns out to matter, so we vary it.
\textsc{none} has no system turn, so the grammar arrives unannounced.
\textsc{neutral} adds a system turn with no format content. \textsc{terse}
and \textsc{schema} ask for JSON explicitly. Intervals are $95\%$ percentile
bootstrap over prompts, $10{,}000$ resamples, both arms resampled at their design
sizes.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{The model is most settled where the masked distribution looks least
settled.}
On harmful prompts the model's own entropy runs $0.06$ to $0.21$ bits
(Table~\ref{tab:main}). That forces the top token above $96\%$ of the mass.
$P_{\text{refuse}}$ says what the mass is on, at $0.77$ and $0.83$ under
\textsc{none}, and without a grammar both models open with a refusal on $88\%$ of
these prompts. Benign prompts leave the model less settled, at $0.76$ to $0.93$
bits. The masked distribution reverses that ordering in every cell.

\paragraph{Read separately, one entropy falls below chance and the other rises
above it.}
This is the cleanest statement of the effect and it is the lower panel of
Table~\ref{tab:main}. The model's own entropy ranks harmful prompts below benign
ones everywhere, at AUROC $0.125$ to $0.175$, every interval strictly under
$0.5$. The served entropy ranks them above, at $0.613$ to $0.750$, with the
interval excluding $0.5$ in three cells of four. The paired shift
$\Delta H = H_{\text{post}} - H_{\text{pre}}$ summarises both and separates the
arms at $0.845$ to $0.916$, better than either reading alone
(Figure~\ref{fig:slope}).

\paragraph{The ordering reverses between the classes, not prompt by prompt.}
Rank correlation limits what we can claim. Pooled across both arms the two
entropies correlate at Spearman $\rho$ between $-0.13$ and $-0.27$, which is weak,
and a pooled coefficient over two clusters mostly restates the separation above
anyway. Within each arm the correlation is absent, between $-0.27$ and $+0.17$
with every interval containing zero. Masking does not so much invert the ordering
of individual prompts as discard it. Knowing the model's entropy on a prompt tells
you little about the entropy that will be served for it. What reverses is the
ordering of the two populations, and that is what a threshold rule acts on.

\paragraph{What is removed does not separate the classes. What survives does.}
The five admitted tokens sit $34.7$ bits below the full vocabulary on Qwen and
$26.9$ on Llama for harmful prompts, so the served distribution renormalises a
remainder the model had all but ruled out. But retained mass is close to
prompt-independent: it separates the arms at AUROC $0.46$ to $0.56$, an interval
containing $0.5$ in all four cells, with medians within a factor of two and an
ordering that is not even consistent across prompt levels. This is unsurprising,
since no instruction-tuned model prefers to open a reply with a brace whatever it
was asked. The amount removed therefore cannot be the discriminator. Two orders of
magnitude separate Llama's retained mass from Qwen's and both show the reversal,
so the effect does not turn on the exact figure, and $H_{\text{post}}$ occupies
between $25\%$ and $61\%$ of its ceiling rather than sitting against it.

\paragraph{The reversal needs an unannounced grammar, and a fresh context.}
Across the eight cells where the prompt does ask for JSON, the ordering reverses
in none. Retained mass is what moves. For Qwen it rises in both arms, to between
$0.28$ and $1.00$, and the served entropy then tracks the model's own. For Llama
it rises on the benign side only, to $0.21$ under \textsc{terse}, while the
harmful arm stays around $10^{-8}$. In that mixed case the model's own
entropy still separates the arms at $0.026$, while the served entropy carries
nothing at all, at $0.499$. The two models exit for different reasons. Llama keeps its refusal
disposition across all four levels, at $P_{\text{refuse}}$ of $0.83$ to $0.89$,
whereas Qwen's falls to $0.00$ under \textsc{schema}. A fixed phrase set reading
zero is also consistent with refusing in words the set misses, so we do not lean
on it. The lexicon-free quantity agrees. Qwen's retained mass on harmful prompts
under \textsc{schema} is $0.84$, so it is plainly emitting the schema rather than
being stopped from refusing.

The effect is confined to the first token as well. Measuring thirty-two forced
positions separately, by the point the schema pins an imperative verb, a median
of twelve tokens in, refusal mass has fallen from $0.77$ to $0.03$ and the
model's own entropy on harmful prompts is now higher than on benign ones. The two
readings agree again. This is what \citet{wei-etal-2023-jailbroken} describe for
forced affirmative prefixes, and it means an entropy averaged over a whole
structured response dilutes the effect rather than compounding it.

Neither quantity detects harm. On XSTest \citep{rottger-etal-2024-xstest}, whose
prompts are alarming but safe, Qwen's served entropy is $1.48$ bits against
$1.26$ on genuinely harmful ones. The served entropy inherits the model's
disposition, over-refusal included.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Entropy read either side of a grammar mask can order inputs oppositely when the
grammar arrives unannounced, driven by the shape of what survives rather than how
much was removed. Between the classes the readings are reversed, within a class
uncorrelated, so no sign flip recovers them. Counterintuitively it is the benign
prompts that look most confident. Compute uncertainty against the model's own
distribution where you can, and report retained mass beside it where you cannot,
since fused sampling kernels discard it first.

\section*{Limitations}

We cover one grammar, one benchmark, fifty prompts per condition, two open-weight
models and a single position, and deeper measurements show the effect does not
survive many forced tokens. At $n\!=\!50$ individual AUROC values should not be
compared, one of four served-entropy intervals touches $0.5$, and
\texttt{bfloat16} inference makes retained masses orders of magnitude only.
Closed-weight systems cannot be examined at all, since the method needs logits
from before the constraint \citep{steindl-etal-2024-linguistic}.
